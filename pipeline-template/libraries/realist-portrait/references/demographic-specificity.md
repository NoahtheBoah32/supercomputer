# Demographic Specificity — how to describe a person concretely

Generators collapse vague identity language toward a single averaged, symmetrical, ~25-year-old face. Specificity is the fix. The goal is a *particular person*, described the way a casting brief describes one — not a type, and not a caricature.

---

## 1. Age — always in years, plus a corroborating detail

Don't write "young" / "middle-aged" / "old". Write `34 years old` and then back it with one age-locating feature from [skin-and-face.md](skin-and-face.md) §3 (e.g. `faint forehead lines only when the brows raise`, `deep nasolabial folds and grey at the temples`). Age + corroboration stops the model splitting the difference.

For children, state age *and* scale cues: `7 years old, milk teeth with one front tooth missing, head large relative to the shoulders`.

## 2. Heritage / ancestry — specific region, not continent or "ambiguous"

"Ethnically ambiguous", "exotic", "mixed" → the averaged face. Instead name a specific ancestry the way a person would describe their own background, and let features follow from it rather than listing stereotyped features:

- `East Asian, Korean heritage` · `South Asian, Tamil heritage` · `West African, Yoruba heritage` · `Northern European, Scandinavian heritage` · `Mediterranean, Southern Italian heritage` · `Andean, Quechua heritage` · `Māori and Pākehā heritage` · `African American` · `Ashkenazi Jewish heritage` · `Levantine Arab heritage`

Mixed heritage: name both and how they read — `Nigerian and Irish heritage, deep brown skin, light hazel eyes, freckling across the nose`.

Then add **individual** variation so it's a person not a template: skin undertone (`warm golden undertone`, `cool olive undertone`, `ruddy`, `neutral`), and one or two specific features (`a broad low nose bridge`, `deep-set eyes with a pronounced brow`, `a strong jaw with a slight underbite`, `full lips with a sharp cupid's bow`, `monolid eyes`, `epicanthic fold`, `a Roman nose with a dorsal bump`).

Describe skin tone in plain concrete terms (`deep brown`, `dark tan`, `light brown`, `fair with pink undertones`, `pale`, `medium with olive cast`) rather than food metaphors.

## 3. Sex & gender presentation

State sex where it matters for anatomy (`man` / `woman` / `non-binary person`) and, separately, presentation if it's not the default assumption (`androgynous presentation, no makeup, short hair`, `trans woman, six months on HRT`, `femme presentation`). Don't let one imply the other.

## 4. Build / body type — name it, generators default to slim-athletic

`heavy-set, soft midsection, full face and neck` · `lean and wiry, visible tendons in the neck and hands` · `broad-shouldered and muscular, thick neck` · `petite and slight, narrow shoulders` · `tall with a slight stoop` · `average build, a little soft` · `post-partum body` · `wheelchair user, [describe posture and how the chair sits in frame]`.

Face-level build cues matter in a portrait crop: `a double chin when the head drops`, `hollow cheeks`, `a full round face`, `prominent cheekbones with hollows beneath`.

## 5. Distinguishing, lived-in details (1–3, not a list of 10)

Vitiligo patches · a cleft chin · a lazy eye · a broken-and-healed nose · heterochromia · a birthmark on the jaw · gapped front teeth · a receding hairline with a strong widow's peak · burn-scar texture on one cheek · gauged earlobes · hand tattoos creeping up the wrist into frame · a hearing aid · a nose ring.

## 6. Region/culture-specific styling (optional, keep it accurate and non-costumey)

If the brief calls for it: name the specific item correctly (`a hijab in plain grey jersey, pinned at the shoulder` not "traditional headscarf"; `a dastar / Sikh turban in navy`; `a Star of David pendant`). Everyday, worn, specific — not festival dress unless the brief is a festival.

## 7. Anti-patterns / bias traps

- "Beautiful", "attractive", "handsome", "stunning", "gorgeous" as the *primary* descriptor → averaged, symmetric, retouched. Drop them; describe the actual face.
- Defaulting every unspecified person to young, slim, light-skinned, able-bodied, symmetrical. If the brief doesn't specify, still *choose* concrete values rather than leaving them blank.
- Pairing a heritage with a laundry list of stereotyped features — name the ancestry, add individual variation, let the model do the rest.
- Costume-y "traditional/tribal/ethnic outfit" language — specify the actual garment, contemporary and worn.
- Sexualising features on any subject described as under 18 — not allowed regardless of framing.
